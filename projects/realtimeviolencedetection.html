Real-Time Violence Detection

Spotting violence in a video stream needs both what a frame looks like and how it changes — a pretrained CNN for the first, a bidirectional LSTM for the second.

Approach

Violence is not a property of a single frame. A raised arm is a punch or a wave depending entirely on what came before it, so a per-frame classifier is working with half the information.

The team's model splits the problem: MobileNetV2, pretrained, acts as a spatial feature extractor on each frame, and a bidirectional LSTM learns the temporal relations across the resulting sequence. MobileNet keeps the per-frame cost low enough to stay real-time, and running the LSTM in both directions lets the classification of any moment draw on frames either side of it. The design targeted three things at once: generality across scenes, accuracy, and fast response.

Two ways to read a video

Two prediction modes were implemented and compared. Frame by frame classifies each frame as it arrives, which is what a live surveillance feed needs and what makes the latency figure meaningful. Whole video considers the full clip at once, trading responsiveness for a decision that sees the entire temporal context.

Limitations

The MobileNetV2–BiLSTM variant gave the best results on the dataset used here, but that dataset is the limit of what the number means. The model still needs validation on standard benchmarks, and two cases in particular are unresolved:

  • Many-to-many and one-to-many altercations, where several interactions overlap in one frame.
  • Scenes involving weapons, or crowd dynamics complex enough that motion alone is ambiguous.
